The honest picture first

Ontario has only a small number of clearly documented tiny-home communities, and they are not all open to the public — several are supportive housing for eligible residents only. For most Ontarians, the realistic tiny-home path is not a community you join, but a backyard additional unit (garden suite, laneway suite or coach house) on a lot, built to the Ontario Building Code with a municipal permit. This site covers both paths without blurring them.

What a tiny home actually costs in Ontario

Published builder prices for Ontario in 2025–2026 range from about $39,000 for a basic 20-foot unit to $195,000+ turnkey, but the all-in cost is what matters: package + foundation + servicing + permits + land. On a serviced lot, a small prefab all-in commonly lands at $110,000–$165,000 excluding land; on an unserviced rural lot, well, septic, hydro and site work add $80,000–$150,000. Frequently asked questions

Are tiny homes legal in Ontario?

Yes — if the home is at least 188 sq ft, has a living/sleeping area, a bathroom with shower or bath, and a cooking area with running water, complies with the Ontario Building Code, and has a municipal building permit. Rules still vary by municipality.

Can I put a tiny home on wheels on my own land?

Usually no. Most Ontario municipalities treat homes on wheels as mobile homes, allowed mainly in mobile home parks or campgrounds. Even land you own usually cannot take one unless your municipality explicitly allows it.

What is the easiest legal route?

A foundation-built additional unit (garden suite, laneway suite, coach house) on a residential lot. Bill 23 reforms allow up to three units on most lots, Ottawa allows four, and development charges are waived for qualifying garden suites.

Can a tiny home be a year-round primary residence?

Yes, if it is certified and built right — CSA Z240 MH or CSA A277 factory certification on a permanent foundation. A build certified only as an RV (CSA Z240 RV) cannot be a primary residence, no matter what it cost.
